About

I'm a scholar working at the intersection of literary studies, cultural history, and computational methods. My work asks what happens when we bring the tools of computer science — network analysis, machine learning, text mining — to bear on the humanistic questions we've always cared most about.

At Morningside University I teach courses in both the CS and English departments, and I'm endlessly interested in building bridges between them. I believe that learning to code is a humanistic act, and that close reading is a form of debugging.

Projects

Current and recent work at the intersection of computation and culture.

The Midwest Novel Network

A network analysis of character co-occurrence across 400 Midwestern novels, 1890–1960. Interactive visualization built with D3.js and a Python NLP pipeline.

Python NLP D3.js Network Analysis

Sioux City Voices Archive

A community digital archive of oral histories from Sioux City, Iowa, built in partnership with the public library. TEI-encoded transcripts with geospatial metadata.

TEI/XML Oral History Archival Leaflet.js

Sentiment Across Centuries

Longitudinal sentiment analysis of American poetry anthologies, tracking emotional valence and topic drift from 1800 to 2000 using fine-tuned transformer models.

BERT Sentiment Poetry HuggingFace

Open Syllabus Visualizer

A tool for visualizing curriculum data from the Open Syllabus Project, revealing canonical and under-taught texts in liberal arts education nationwide.

React Data Viz Open Data

GenreLens

A browser extension that classifies the genre of any webpage text using a lightweight on-device ML model. Built as a teaching demonstration for my DH methods course.

TensorFlow.js Browser Extension Pedagogy

Letters to the Prairie

A digital edition of 19th-century letters by settlers on the Iowa prairie, encoded in TEI with contextual annotations, maps, and a timeline interface.

TEI Digital Edition IIIF Omeka
